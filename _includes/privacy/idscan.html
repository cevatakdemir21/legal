Scanning IDs: the camera and what is read

{{ page.app_name }} is used by a person checking IDs, for example at the door of a venue. This section explains what happens to the information on an ID that is scanned, both for the person using the App and for the person whose ID it is.

  • Camera. The camera is used only to read the barcode on the back of a driver license or ID card, or the two or three machine-readable lines on a passport or passport card. iOS asks for permission first, and it can be withdrawn at any time in Settings → {{ page.app_name }} → Camera. Reading happens on the device. Camera images and video are never saved and are never sent anywhere.
  • What is read. A barcode or passport line contains more than the App needs. The App reads it in memory, works out the age and whether the ID has expired, and then discards the rest.
  • What is saved on the device. For each scan: the name, the gender shown on the ID, the age in years, the result of the check, the minimum age that was set, the way the ID was read, the type of document, the issuing state, province or country, the expiration date, and the date and time of the scan.
  • What is never saved. The address, the photo, the date of birth, the ID or passport number, the raw barcode or passport lines, and any camera image.
  • Recognising a returning guest. To count visits and to match the VIP and Banned lists, the App keeps a one-way code (a salted SHA-256 hash) made from the ID number and the issuer. The number cannot be read back from the code. The random value used to make the code is created on the device, kept in the device’s keychain, and never leaves it, so the code means nothing on any other device.
  • VIP and Banned lists and notes. If the person using the App marks a guest as VIP or Banned, or writes a note, the App stores that code together with the name, the gender, the note, the date added and the date last seen. These entries stay until they are removed in the App.
  • Entering a date of birth by hand. The date typed is used once to work out the age and is not saved. The saved record has no name and no code.

How long scans are kept, and removing them

  • Saved scans are deleted automatically after {{ page.idscan_retention_days | default: 90 }} days. This period can be changed in the App’s settings, including to keep scans until they are deleted by hand.
  • Save History can be turned off in the App’s settings. New scans are then checked and shown, but not saved.
  • Delete All History in the App’s settings removes every saved scan at once. Scans from a chosen period can be deleted from the History screen.
  • A guest is removed from the VIP or Banned list by swiping the entry in that list.
  • Deleting the App removes all of the above from the device. The random value in the keychain may remain on the device afterwards. It contains no information about anyone.

Exporting and sharing

The person using the App can export the scan history as a CSV file. The file contains the date, time, name, gender, age, minimum age, result, method, document type and issuer for each scan, and the App warns that it includes names before creating it. A summary of the statistics, which contains counts only and no names, can also be shared. Either goes to the app or person chosen, for example Mail, Messages, AirDrop or Files. It does not pass through us, and from that moment the recipient’s own terms and privacy practices apply.

If you email us from the App

Contact Support and Report a Problem open an email draft that you can read and edit before sending. The draft contains the App’s version, the iOS version and the device model. A problem report also contains the result of the scan, the way it was read, the document type, the issuer and the barcode version. It never contains a name, an age, a code or the time of a scan.

If your ID was scanned with {{ page.app_name }}

The record of your scan is held on the device of the person or business that scanned it, not by us. We cannot see it, change it or delete it. To ask what is held about you, or to ask for it to be deleted, contact that person or business. They decide why IDs are scanned and how long records are kept, and they are responsible for following the laws that apply where they operate.
