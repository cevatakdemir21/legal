Scanning IDs: the camera and what is read

{{ page.app_name }} is used by a person checking IDs, for example at the door of a venue. This section explains what happens to the information on an ID that is scanned, both for the person using the App and for the person whose ID it is.

  • Camera. The camera is used only to read the barcode on the back of a driver license or ID card, or the two or three machine-readable lines on a passport or passport card. iOS asks for permission first, and it can be withdrawn at any time in Settings → {{ page.app_name }} → Camera. Reading happens on the device. Camera images and video are never saved and are never sent anywhere.
  • What is read. A barcode or passport line contains more than the App needs. The App reads it in memory, works out the age, whether the ID has expired and whether its data is consistent, and then discards the rest.
  • What is saved on the device. For each scan: the name, the gender shown on the ID, the date of birth, the age in years, the result of the check and the reasons for it, the minimum age that was set, the way the ID was read, the type of ID, the issuing state, province or country, the issue and expiration dates, the barcode version, and the date and time of the scan. The App also keeps a simplified form of the name, so that it can notice when the same ID number is scanned with a different name.
  • What is never saved. The address, the photo, the ID or passport number, the raw barcode or passport lines, and any camera image.
  • Recognising a returning guest. To count visits, to match the VIP and Banned lists and to notice the same ID number with a different name, the App keeps a one-way code (a salted SHA-256 hash) made from the ID number and the issuer. The number cannot be read back from the code. The random value used to make the code is created on the device and kept in the device’s keychain{% if page.services contains 'icloud' %}, or in iCloud Keychain while iCloud sync is on (see “iCloud sync” below){% endif %}. It never reaches us, so the code means nothing to anyone else.
  • VIP and Banned lists and notes. If the person using the App marks a guest as VIP or Banned, or writes a note, the App stores that code together with the name, the gender, the note, the date added and the date last seen. These entries stay until they are removed in the App.
  • Entering a date of birth by hand. The date typed is saved with the age and the result, like a scan, but the record has no name and no code.

How long scans are kept, and removing them

  • Saved scans are deleted automatically after {{ page.idscan_retention_days | default: 90 }} days. VIP and Banned entries are not affected.
  • A single scan can be deleted at any time by swiping it in the History screen or from its details screen. Scans from today, the last 7 days, the last 30 days or all time can be deleted together from the History screen.
  • A guest is removed from the VIP or Banned list by swiping the entry in that list.
  • Deleting the App removes all of the above from the device{% if page.services contains 'icloud' %}; copies in iCloud are covered under “iCloud sync” below{% endif %}. The random value in the keychain may remain on the device afterwards. It contains no information about anyone.

Exporting and sharing

The person using the App can export the scans shown in History as a CSV file. The file contains the date, time, name, gender, age, minimum age, result, method, ID type and issuer for each scan. A summary of the statistics, which contains counts only and no names, can also be shared. Either goes to the app or person chosen, for example Mail, Messages, AirDrop or Files. It does not pass through us, and from that moment the recipient’s own terms and privacy practices apply.

If you email us from the App

Contact Support and Request a Feature open an email draft that you can read and edit before sending. The draft contains the App’s version, the iOS version and the device model. It never contains a scan, a name, an age or a code.

If your ID was scanned with {{ page.app_name }}

The record of your scan is held on the device of the person or business that scanned it{% if page.services contains 'icloud' %}, and in their own iCloud account if they turned on iCloud sync{% endif %}, not by us. We cannot see it, change it or delete it. Nothing that identifies you is sent to us or to the analytics and crash-reporting services named in this policy. To ask what is held about you, or to ask for it to be deleted, contact that person or business. They decide why IDs are scanned, and they are responsible for following the laws that apply where they operate.
